Applying for a first-time Vietnamese passport is not simply a matter of filling out a form and submitting it. For many overseas Vietnamese — particularly those who left Vietnam many years ago or who have since naturalized in another country — the Vietnamese diplomatic mission may need to verify nationality, cross-check civil status records, or request additional documents proving a legal connection to Vietnam.

For this reason, preparing a complete and accurate file from the outset can significantly reduce processing time and the risk of the application being returned for supplementary documents.

# What is the Difference Between Vietnamese Nationality Confirmation and a Vietnamese Passport?

**Confirmation of Vietnamese nationality** is the process by which the competent authority reviews, cross-checks, and evaluates the documents submitted by an applicant in order to determine whether that person currently holds Vietnamese nationality under the Law on Vietnamese Nationality. This is not a "naturalization" procedure — it is a verification of an individual's existing nationality status based on available records, documents, and civil status data, used as the basis for deciding whether a Vietnamese passport may be issued.

For overseas Vietnamese, nationality confirmation is commonly required in the following situations:

- Never having been issued a Vietnamese passport.
- Having left Vietnam many years ago.
- No longer holding any Vietnamese personal identification documents.
- Having naturalized as a citizen of another country.
- Discrepancies in name or date of birth between different documents.
- A child born abroad to a Vietnamese father or mother.

In many cases, the Vietnamese diplomatic mission in Canada must coordinate with the competent authorities in Vietnam to verify information before deciding whether to issue a passport.

A **Vietnamese passport** is a document issued by the competent Vietnamese authority to a Vietnamese citizen for the purpose of exit and entry, and it also serves as proof of nationality and identity for the holder in international transactions.

For Vietnamese people living abroad, a Vietnamese passport is not only a travel document — it also carries several important rights, such as:

- Entering Vietnam as a Vietnamese citizen without needing to apply for a visa.
- Facilitating administrative, civil status, and civil procedures in Vietnam.
- Supporting transactions relating to housing, real estate, investment, business, and inheritance under Vietnamese law.
- Serving as key supporting evidence for procedures such as birth registration for a child, marriage registration, identity confirmation, or other matters involving a foreign element.

For many long-term overseas Vietnamese residents, obtaining a Vietnamese passport also carries the significance of maintaining a legal connection with their homeland, making it easier to live, work, or invest in Vietnam in the future.

#### Who Can Apply for a First-Time Vietnamese Passport?

Not every case follows the same process. Depending on nationality status, place of birth, and residency history, each applicant's file may have different requirements.

Generally, the following groups may be eligible to apply for a first-time Vietnamese passport:

- **Persons born in Vietnam who have never been issued a passport:** This is the most common group. Many people left Vietnam as children and later settled in Canada, the United States, Australia, or Europe without ever obtaining a Vietnamese passport. If they still hold Vietnamese nationality, or have not lost it under the law, they may be considered for passport issuance.
- **Persons with a Vietnamese citizen parent:** In many cases, a child born abroad may have grounds to establish Vietnamese nationality through a Vietnamese citizen father or mother, depending on the Law on Vietnamese Nationality and the status of their birth registration and civil records.
- **Persons who need to confirm their Vietnamese nationality:** This is a relatively complex scenario. An applicant may no longer have a birth certificate, household registration book, or other personal identification, having left Vietnam many years ago. In such cases, confirming Vietnamese nationality is typically an essential step before the competent authority will consider issuing a passport.

#### Why Do Some Applications Take Months, or Even Years?

Many people assume that obtaining a passport only takes a few weeks. However, for first-time applications by overseas Vietnamese, processing time can be considerably longer if the diplomatic mission needs to verify information with authorities in Vietnam. Common reasons include:

- Inconsistencies between information on the birth certificate and the foreign passport.
- A change of name after naturalizing in another country.
- Missing documents proving Vietnamese nationality.
- No civil status records remaining at the local level in Vietnam.
- Discrepancies in date of birth between different documents.
- An incomplete file at the time of initial submission.

This is also why many clients choose to use a professional support service to have their file reviewed before submission, reducing the risk of complications and saving considerable processing time.

# Documents That May Help Prove Vietnamese Nationality

Depending on the case, an applicant may use one or more of the following documents to support nationality verification:

1. **Vietnamese birth certificate:** This is one of the most important documents for establishing an applicant's identity and origin. If the original or a valid copy is still available, the review process is generally more straightforward.
2. **Vietnamese ID card (CMND or CCCD):** If previously issued before leaving Vietnam, this document carries significant weight in supporting proof of nationality and identity.
3. **Previous Vietnamese passport:** If a Vietnamese passport was previously issued — even if it has since expired or been cancelled — it remains a highly valuable document for verification purposes.
4. **Household registration book (Sổ hộ khẩu) or prior residency documents:** Although the paper household registration book is no longer in use in Vietnam, for older files it can still be an important source of information for cross-checking.
5. **Other civil status documents:** These documents can help the competent authority establish identity when primary documents have been lost.

- Civil status correction decisions.
- Marriage certificate.
- Birth certificate extract.
- Death certificate of the applicant's father or mother.
- Locally held civil status records.

#### What if No Vietnamese Documents Remain at All?

This is a fairly common situation for Vietnamese people who have lived abroad for decades.

Many clients have only:

- A Canadian passport.
- A Canadian citizenship certificate.
- A foreign birth certificate.
- An expired permanent resident card or other foreign documents.

This does not mean a Vietnamese passport cannot be obtained. Depending on the circumstances, it is still possible to gather or search for supporting information from various sources — such as civil status records, archived data, or documents related to the applicant's parents and family in Vietnam. However, such files typically require more thorough preparation and may take longer to process.

# How Long Does Processing Take?

This is a question of concern to most clients. In reality, there is no fixed processing time that applies to every case, since each file has its own legal circumstances and level of complexity.

Processing time may be affected by several factors, including:

- Whether nationality confirmation is required.
- Whether the file was complete from the outset.
- Whether civil status records in Vietnam need to be located.
- Whether there are discrepancies or inconsistencies between documents.
- The volume of applications the competent authority is processing at any given time.

For this reason, applicants should plan ahead, especially if the passport is needed for purposes such as travel to Vietnam, investment, resolving an inheritance matter, marriage, or other civil transactions.

Based on our experience assisting clients, the following types of files typically require longer processing times:

- Applicants who left Vietnam a very long time ago and have lived abroad for decades may no longer have complete archived records, or may require cross-checking against multiple data sources.
- A change of name after naturalizing abroad is a common reason for requests for explanation or supplementary documents.
- Missing birth certificates or lost civil status documents may require additional steps to obtain extracts or verify information.
- Files containing inconsistent information (for example, differing dates of birth, places of birth, name spellings, or incorrect parents' names) require clarification before the competent authority can make a decision.
